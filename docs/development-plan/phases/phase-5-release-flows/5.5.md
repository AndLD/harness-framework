# 5.5 — Executors in the runtime-adapter contract, and the reuse of a worker session

**Status:** 🟡 · **Agent:** C3 · **Size:** 8 SP · **Needs:** 5.2

**Executors in the runtime-adapter contract, and the reuse of a worker session**: the orchestrator starts each step through an executor of the runtime adapter. `session`, the default for task, cut and consumer steps: a session of its own that the orchestrator controls, with the hooks and the CI monitoring of a session, which the owner can open and steer. `subagent`, for short analysis steps: in the orchestrator's process, the full result comes back, but it gets no CI wake-ups. `manual`, the fallback of a runtime without automation and what the owner does today: the orchestrator prints the prompt and the owner pastes the report. (1) The adapter contract (design section 4) gains: start a step with a model and an effort; continue a worker with a next prompt; fetch the worker's full final report (a session's notice may be cut short); read a session's context size. A change of a contract needs the owner's approval in the pull request. (2) Mappings: claude-code (the desktop app's sessions, the command line `claude -p` with `--resume`, subagents); cursor `manual` only, a stub with no real implementation. (3) **`reuse: auto`**: the next step goes to the previous step's worker only when the repository, the model and the effort are the same; the steps are related (the same release, or the next needs the previous); the worker's context is below a threshold (default 40% of its window); no question to the owner and no wait for a usage reset comes between them; and the worker has run fewer than three steps. Otherwise a fresh worker starts. The reason is the prompt cache: a fresh session still reads the shared prefix (system prompt, tools, entry file) from the cache and pays for its orientation reads, while a reused one reads its whole context from the cache only while the cache lives (5 minutes to 1 hour, depending on the runtime); after that the long context is written again at full price, which can cost more than a fresh start, and a change of model or effort drops the cache anyway. A small step after a related bigger one may keep the bigger step's model (a logged D0 decision). Verified by the conformance suite on both runtime adapters and on broken copies (an adapter without an executor), by tests of the reuse rule (each condition that forbids it), and by a probe of a two-step flow run by the session executor, the second step warm.
