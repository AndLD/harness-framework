# 5.2 — Flows: files of the plan that an orchestrator runs, with the procedure `run-flow`

**Status:** ⬜ · **Agent:** C3 · **Size:** 5 SP · **Needs:** 5.1

**Flows: files of the plan that an orchestrator runs, with the procedure `run-flow`**: a flow is an ordered chain of steps (prompts to workers) that delivers one or more releases; an orchestrator agent runs it and keeps its log. Today the owner orchestrates by hand from a local list of prompts. Delivered, for this repository and for the projects that use the framework:

1. **Layout, like phases.** A folder `flows/` beside the plan index (a profile role) with a generated index `README.md` (flow, releases, mode, size, estimated usage, status, link), `flow-<n>-<slug>.md` and its append-only `flow-<n>-<slug>.log.md`. Numbers are identities; the order between flows is a `Needs: flow <n>` line, else the number. A flow declares its releases (`Releases:`); a release belongs to at most one flow, and a release in no flow is not planned to run. One flow may hold every release, for a run from start to end. A finished flow stays in place, as a finished phase does. The plan index links the flows index.
2. **Templates** in `core/plan/`. The settings block: `mode` (brainstorm | seamless, default brainstorm); `askAt` (D1 to D4, default D2); `levels` (the level of a decision kind; defaults: a development cut D1, a final cut or a hotfix D3, answering or closing reports on the host D4); `executor` (session | subagent | manual, default session, overridable per step); `reuse` (auto | never, default auto); `commits` (per-step | per-cut, default per-step); `usage` (the subscription, from the ignored local profile file, and the stop thresholds, default 85% of the 5-hour window and 90% of the weekly one); `repos` (the home repository only, by default). The steps table: `# | Kind | Repo | Scope | Needs | Stops for | SP | Est. usage | Model | State`. The step kinds: `tasks`, `cut`, `consumer-take`, `triage-fix`, `decision`, `custom` (the only kind with a prompt of its own). The scope of a step names ids only, never what a release holds; a step inserted midway takes a suffix id (`6c`) and never renumbers the others, and is a logged decision; a change of what a release holds changes `releases.md` in the same commit. The log entry: a step's start and end, its usage, its report, each decision with its level, the options, the worker's recommendation, the orchestrator's own analysis, the choice and who decided.
3. **The procedure `run-flow`** (a skill too). Decision levels: D0 mechanical, D1 local (a worker's choice inside its task), D2 plan-shaping (add, move or defer a task, insert or split a step, change a step's model, move a release between flows), D3 contract or scope (core rules, adapter contracts, what a release promises its consumers, a dropped feature), D4 external communication; hard limits that no mode crosses: admin bypass, a cut on a red check, the non-negotiables, force-push or deleting a remote branch. Brainstorm asks the owner from `askAt` up and decides below it; seamless decides every level and writes a "review later" digest of its D2 to D4 decisions. The **step report** a worker ends with: status (done, partial, blocked), the pull requests merged and the ids done, the open decisions (each with its options, the worker's recommendation and why, and the level it suggests), deviations, reports filed, the next step. The orchestrator reads the report and the state checks, never the diffs (the migration note before a cut is the one document it reads). **Unbiased analysis:** before it reads a worker's recommendation, the orchestrator writes the question, the options and its own pick from the plan and the design; if the picks differ, its log entry answers the worker's argument. One orchestrator per flow (a "running" log entry with its session is the claim). A failed or partial step is continued once in its session, then becomes a decision. Escalation in any mode: a blocker the worker could not solve, CI still red after the automatic fixes, a step over twice its estimate, work outside the step's scope. The owner gets a digest at every stop and at the end of a flow (done, decided alone, usage, next) and a notification when the flow waits for them. The orchestrator keeps only the flow, the tail of its log and the current report, writes the log and restarts when its context grows, and always between two flows. Its own changes (the flow's state, the log) go by one pull request per step, plan-related and merged as a task's; `commits: per-cut` uses one draft pull request per flow merged at each cut.
4. **Resume phrases** "Run flow N", "Run flows N–M", "Continue flow N" in the profile and the Git rules: the phrase approves what the settings of the named flows allow, and the orchestrator runs them in order and stops after the last.
5. **The design** gets a section on releases and flows.

Waves (parallel steps) are not in scope; the `Needs` column keeps room for them. The tools come in 5.4 to 5.7, the authority to cut and answer in 5.3. Verified by `npm run docs:check` (the CORE scan, the generated skill, the links) and by two probes on a scratch project with a filled flow and the `manual` executor: told "Run flow 1" in brainstorm, the orchestrator decides a D1 question and asks at a D2 one, and logs both with its own analysis before the worker's pick; in seamless it decides both and writes the digest.
