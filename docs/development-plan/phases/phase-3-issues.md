| 3.7 | **A pinned project does not scan its copy of `core/` with its own terms**: in pinned mode `check core` scans nothing, since the framework scans `core/` upstream (`frameworkCoreFolder`). | 3.8 | **A leak found by a consumer has no path into the framework, and blocks that consumer until a release fixes it**: after 3.7 a consumer whose sync finds a word in `core/` cannot take the version, and nothing says how the framework hears of it. | 3.9 | **The claim rule does not say who owns a claim after its session ends**: method §6 says a task with an open pull request "belongs to the session that opened it, and nobody else starts it", and §7 that an agent "never starts a task that has an open pull request"; but §6 also tells a session that continues a phase to go on from the latest "next step", which is often a task that a paused session claimed. Nothing tells a session its own earlier claim from a live claim of another agent. In the closing review of the extraction, three probes read the same state (a draft claim open) two ways: one took the task over, two left it alone (finding H-20261103-02 of AndLD/bot-rts). One rule in method §6 and §7: a claim belongs to its session while that session works; a session that resumes a phase continues a claimed task when the task's log or the phase log records a pause or a "next step" that names it, or when the owner says so; otherwise it asks. The resume step of the procedure `start-task` says the same. Verified by the CORE scan and the generated skills, and by two probes on a scratch project with an open claim: with a log entry that names it as the next step the agent continues it; without one it asks. | C2 | 2 | ⬜ |
| 3.10 | **A finished task is logged twice, and a resuming agent reads the rows of finished tasks**: method §6 and `end-session` say a task writes its own log fragment and a phase-wide matter goes to the phase's work log. In the project this framework was extracted from, 15 of the 23 entries of one phase log restate a task's fragment, and the framework's Phase 1 log does the same (5 of 7). The copies drift. A resuming agent also reads the whole phase file, most of which is the long rows of finished tasks. (1) One source: a finished task writes only its fragment, and its "next step" names what follows; the phase log keeps phase-wide matters only (decisions, pauses, the close of the phase). (2) The hook prints the newest "next step" across the phase log and the fragments, by entry date, and the open tasks of the phase (id and title). (3) `start-task` reads the rows of the open tasks only, not the whole table.
